About the Competition

What Is the Genius Olympiad?

An international high school competition that challenges students to solve real-world environmental problems through science, technology, art, and entrepreneurship.

GENIUS Olympiad is organised by Terra Science and Education, a nonprofit dedicated to educational advancement, and hosted at St. John Fisher University in Rochester, New York. It is one of the most globally diverse science competitions for high school students, drawing participants from over 85 countries and all 50 US states.

ATAST's Role

Tunisia's Gateway to the World Stage

ATAST organises the national qualification process that selects one or two Tunisian teams to represent the country at the International Genius Olympiad.

The national qualifier is a rigorous evaluation process — teams submit their projects and compete for a limited number of coveted spots. Only the strongest and most innovative environmental projects make the cut and earn the right to carry Tunisia's flag to Rochester, New York.

Representing your country at Genius Olympiad is one of the most prestigious achievements available to a Tunisian high school student. Past Tunisian teams have presented projects spanning renewable energy, waste management, water conservation, and environmental AI — standing alongside the world's best young innovators.

Project Categories

9 Disciplines, One Environmental Mission

Projects must address real environmental challenges. The competition accepts a wide range of formats so that every talented student — scientist, artist, coder, or entrepreneur — can compete.

Artificial Intelligence

Machine learning and AI solutions applied to environmental monitoring, conservation, and prediction.

Art

Visual art, illustration, and mixed media that raises awareness of environmental issues and drives change.

Coding

Software applications, tools, and platforms that tackle environmental problems through smart programming.

Entrepreneurship

Sustainable business concepts and ventures that offer scalable, market-driven solutions to environmental challenges.

Music

Original compositions and performances that communicate environmental advocacy through the power of sound.

Robotics

Engineering and research using robotic systems to monitor ecosystems, automate cleanup, or solve environmental problems.

Science

Scientific research, experiments, and engineering innovations that directly address environmental issues.

Short Film

Documentary and narrative films that promote behavioral change and spark conversation about environmental topics.

Speech

Persuasive oral presentations that advocate for environmental awareness, policy change, or community action.

How It Works

From Idea to the International Stage

01

Develop Your Project

Build an environmental project in one of nine disciplines. It should propose a concrete solution or communicate a meaningful environmental message.

02

National Qualifier via ATAST

Submit your project through ATAST's national selection process. Judges evaluate projects on innovation, environmental impact, and presentation quality.

03

Selection for Tunisia

The top one or two teams are selected to officially represent Tunisia. ATAST submits the accepted projects to the international competition.

04

Video Presentation

Finalists submit a video presentation (9–12 minutes depending on category) to an international panel of 5–10 expert reviewers.

05

Travel to Rochester, NY

Selected teams travel to St. John Fisher University in Rochester, New York, for the June finals — an unforgettable international experience.

06

Awards Ceremony

Projects are recognised with medals, certificates, and scholarships. All participants receive official recognition — only the best take home Gold.

University Scholarships at RIT

Award winners receive renewable annual scholarships for enrolment at the Rochester Institute of Technology — one of the top US universities for science, engineering, and technology. Scholarships are available to international students and are contingent on RIT admission. Even participants who don't win a medal receive a $12,000 scholarship.

Eligibility & Requirements

Who Can Apply?

The competition is open to all Tunisian high school students. There is no minimum grade requirement — what matters is the quality and environmental relevance of your project.

  • Currently enrolled high school student
  • Project must address a real-world environmental issue
  • Any of the 9 disciplines: Science, AI, Coding, Robotics, Art, Music, Short Film, Speech, Entrepreneurship
  • Individual or team projects accepted
  • Projects judged on innovation, environmental impact, and presentation
  • Teams under 18 require an adult chaperone when travelling

Beyond the Competition

An Experience That Goes Beyond a Medal

Representing Tunisia at Genius Olympiad is about far more than winning — it's a life-defining international experience.

Participants meet peers from 85+ countries, explore one of America's most vibrant university campuses, and attend events including a trip to Niagara Falls, an International Culture Fair, and an RIT open house for prospective students.

Optional trips to Washington DC, New York City, and Boston/Ivy League universities allow students to extend their American experience. Award-winning science projects are also eligible for publication in the International Journal of High School Research, giving students a genuine peer-reviewed credential before university.
